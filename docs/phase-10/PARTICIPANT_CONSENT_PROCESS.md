# Recruit and consent playtest participants

Phase 10 cannot begin until this process is approved. It covers a **private formative
pilot** with 5 to 8 children aged 8 through 12, run by the project team. It is not a
public study and produces no published data set.

This document is not legal advice. A privacy or legal reviewer must approve it against
the launch jurisdictions named in DR-06 before the first session.

## Who takes part

- Children aged 8 through 12, recruited only through parents, guardians, or teachers
  already known to the team. No public advertising, no open sign-up form.
- Cover both ends of the age range, mixed typing experience, and where possible at
  least one participant who uses an access need relevant to the
  [child-safety checklist](../phase-1/CHILD_SAFETY_PRIVACY_CHECKLIST.md).
- Target 5 to 8 sessions, matching the DR-04 minimum for accepting the first-zone
  reading and teaching sequence.

## Two-part permission

Consent comes from the guardian. Assent comes from the child. Both are required, and
either can be withdrawn at any moment without giving a reason.

### Guardian consent, collected before the session

The guardian is given the participant information sheet below and confirms, in writing
(email is sufficient for a private pilot):

- They have read what happens in the session and what is recorded.
- They agree to their child taking part.
- They understand they can stop the session or request deletion at any time.
- They know who to contact.

Record only: participant code, date of consent, and the fact consent was given. Do not
store the guardian's message, name, or address with the session notes.

### Child assent, collected at the start of the session

Read aloud, in these words or simpler:

> We are testing a game, not testing you. There are no right answers and nothing you do
> can break it. I will watch what you do and write some notes. If you want to stop, say
> "stop" or just close the laptop, and we stop straight away. Is that okay?

Record the answer as a yes or no on the observation sheet. Do not ask a child to sign
anything. If the child hesitates, is unsure, or goes quiet, treat it as a no and stop.

## What is recorded, and what is never recorded

Recorded:

- A participant code only: **P01**, **P02**, and so on.
- Age in years and the device and input used.
- Screen capture of the game window, and optionally audio of what the child says.
- The moderator's written observations and the exported save file.

Never recorded, requested, or stored:

- Name, school, address, location, contact detail, or anything identifying.
- The child's face or any camera feed.
- Anything the game itself does not already store locally.

The game collects nothing on its own. Everything in this pilot is captured by the
moderator, deliberately and visibly.

## Handling the data

| Item | Where it lives | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Screen and audio recordings | Team-controlled encrypted storage, access limited to the session moderators | Delete within 30 days of the playtest report being accepted |
| Observation sheets and issue list | Repository under `docs/evidence/phase-10/`, anonymized to participant codes | Keep |
| Exported save files | Team-controlled encrypted storage, filename is the participant code | Delete with the recordings |
| Consent record | Separate from session notes; code, date, and confirmation only | Delete 12 months after the pilot |

Nothing from a session leaves the team. No recording, note, or save file is shared with
a third party, uploaded to an external service, or used in marketing.

A guardian who asks for deletion gets every item for that participant code removed,
including anonymized notes if they ask for that too. Confirm in writing when it is done.

## Running the session safely

- A guardian is present or within sight and earshot for the whole session. Never leave a
  moderator alone with a child.
- Maximum 45 minutes in the room, maximum 30 minutes of hands-on play, with an offered
  break at the halfway point.
- Stop immediately, without finishing the task list, if the child asks, shows distress or
  fatigue, or hits an accessibility blocker that prevents a required action.
- The moderator never says a child is wrong, slow, or failing. Confusion is a finding
  about the game, and the moderator says so out loud: "that tells me we should make this
  clearer."
- If a child says something that raises a welfare concern, stop the session and follow the
  team's safeguarding escalation. Do not record the detail in the observation sheet.

## Participant information sheet

Give this to the guardian before asking for consent.

> **What is it?** CodeQuest 3D is an offline coding game for children aged 8 to 12. Your
> child writes small pieces of real JavaScript to move a character through a 3D world.
>
> **What happens?** Your child plays the first few missions on our laptop for up to 30
> minutes while we watch where the game confuses them. We may ask what they think a
> button does. We are testing the game, not your child.
>
> **What do you record?** The game screen, and what your child says if you agree to audio.
> We never record faces and never ask for your child's name, school, or any contact
> detail. Notes use a code such as P01.
>
> **Does the game keep anything?** The game stores progress and code on that one laptop
> and sends nothing anywhere. It has no accounts, adverts, chat, or sharing.
>
> **Can we stop?** Yes, at any point, without giving a reason. You can also ask us to
> delete everything from your child's session afterwards, and we will confirm when it is
> done.
>
> **Who do I contact?** _[name and contact for the project owner]_
